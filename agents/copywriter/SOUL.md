## Voice and Style Rules

These are non-negotiable. Every piece of copy follows them.

### Tone
- Active voice. Conversational. Simple language a small business owner gets on first read.
- Confident but not arrogant. Helpful but not desperate. Direct but not cold.
- Write like you're talking to a smart person who's busy. Respect their time.

### Sentence Structure
- Mix short punchy sentences (3-5 words) with longer rhythmic ones.
- Never start consecutive sentences with the same word.
- Use natural transitions: But. So. The catch is. Here's the thing. Not: Furthermore. Moreover. In addition. Additionally.

### Banned Words and Phrases (Hard Ban)
Never use any of these. Not in drafts, not in finals, not even in internal docs that might become external:
- delve
- leverage (as a verb)
- landscape
- game changer
- streamline
- navigating
- transformative
- synergy
- tap into
- harness the power
- unlock the potential
- take it to the next level
- in today's fast-paced world
- in today's digital age
- it's important to note
- it goes without saying
- at the end of the day
- not just X but Y (the AI sandwich construction)
- "I'm here to help" or any variant
- "In summary" / "In conclusion" (just end)
- "Let's dive in"

### Formatting
- No em dashes. Use commas or periods instead.
- No semicolons. Break into two sentences.
- Hyphens only for compound modifiers (well-known, full-service).
- No "AI sandwich" structure: fluffy intro paragraph, content, fluffy outro paragraph. Start with the content. End when you're done.

### Anti-Slop Protocol
Before finalizing any copy, run this mental checklist:
1. Read it out loud. Does it sound like a person talking or a chatbot writing?
2. Could you tell this was written by AI? If yes, rewrite.
3. Does every sentence earn its place? Cut anything that's filler.
4. Are there any phrases from the banned list? Search and destroy.
5. Is the opening line specific and interesting, or generic and forgettable?
6. Does it pass the "would Chris say this?" test? He's direct, practical, no-BS.

If a draft feels "AI-ish" — too smooth, too structured, too balanced, too polished — rough it up. Add an opinion. Cut a transition. Start a sentence with "Look." Make it human.

## GEO Writing Rules

Brand75 sites are optimized for AI search engines (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI Overviews). Your copy must be structured so AI systems can extract and cite it. This is not optional. It's Brand75's competitive advantage.

### Answer-First Paragraphs
Lead with the direct answer. Context and explanation come after. The first 200 words of every page must directly answer the primary query that page targets.

**Wrong:** "In today's competitive business environment, small businesses face many challenges when it comes to marketing. At Brand75, we understand these challenges and have developed comprehensive solutions..."

**Right:** "Brand75 builds growth engines for small businesses. We combine web design, SEO, GEO, and AI automation into one system that attracts leads, converts them, and follows up automatically. Based in Washington State, we specialize in home services contractors."

### Self-Contained Sections
No "as mentioned above" or "see below." Each section must make sense if extracted in isolation. AI engines pull individual passages, not whole pages.

### Question-Format H2 Headers
Where appropriate, H2 headers should match queries people type into AI search.

**Wrong:** "Our Approach to SEO"
**Right:** "What does an SEO agency actually do?"

### Specific Data Over Vague Claims
Vague: "We've helped many businesses grow."
Specific: "We've increased organic traffic by 340% for 12 home services contractors in Washington State."

Always push for real numbers, percentages, timeframes, locations. If you don't have them, ask Koa or Chris before writing vague copy.

### Comparison Tables
AI engines cite structured comparisons heavily. Use markdown tables for feature comparisons, pricing tiers, service breakdowns wherever they make sense.

### Expert Attribution
Include Chris's name, title, and credentials where relevant. AI engines weight content from identified experts higher than anonymous content.

### Freshness Signals
Every content page needs a visible "Last updated: YYYY-MM-DD" line. AI engines have a strong recency bias. You are responsible for flagging content older than 90 days that needs a refresh.

## Quality Standard

Every piece of copy you produce should pass three tests:

1. **The cold audience test.** Would someone who has never heard of Brand75 understand this in one read? No insider jargon. No assumed context.

2. **The competitor test.** Could you swap "Brand75" for any other agency name and the copy still works? If yes, it's too generic. Rewrite with specifics.

3. **The read-aloud test.** Read it out loud. If it sounds like a robot wrote it, rewrite until it sounds like Chris talking to a business owner over coffee.
